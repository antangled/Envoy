# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: people who win work through cold outreach and do it by hand today. Student consulting clubs sourcing semester projects (the founding user runs outreach for BEACN at UC Berkeley), small agencies and consultancies pitching clients, and founders proposing partnerships. They work at a laptop in daylight, often between classes or meetings, in 20 to 40 minute sessions, and they care about not embarrassing themselves in someone's inbox.

Secondary: hackathon judges evaluating the product from the signed-out landing page and a recorded demo.

## Product Purpose

Envoy turns a plain-English outreach goal into a short list of organizations worth approaching, a one-page brief on each with clickable sources, about ten real people per company, and a draft email in the sender's own template. The sender reviews and edits every draft and sends from their own Gmail, spaced and capped. Success is a campaign that takes minutes instead of an evening per company, where every claim in the email can be traced to a page.

## Positioning

Outreach with receipts: every sentence Envoy writes about a company links to the page it came from, and what it could not verify is listed instead of guessed.

## Conversion & proof

Applies to the signed-out landing page only.

- Primary CTA: Continue with Google. Secondary: Try the demo (no account needed).
- The line a visitor remembers: every sentence has a source.
- Belief ladder: this is the manual outreach chore I already do; the research is real and checkable; my own email stays mine; it sends like a person, from my Gmail; it costs cents per company.
- Proof on hand: measured cost per company from real runs; a real sourced draft (Lightship example). No testimonials or customer logos yet, so none are shown.

## Brand Personality

Courteous, exact, unhurried. An envoy carries a letter of introduction backed by a dossier: formal enough to be trusted, warm enough to be read. Copy is plain, specific, and never salesy; claims are measured and stated once.

## Anti-references

Spray-and-pray sales tools (aggressive gradients, "10x your pipeline", growth-hacker tone). Generic SaaS card grids with icon-heading-text tiles. Fake social proof. Dark, neon "AI" aesthetics.

## Design Principles

- Show the receipt: wherever Envoy makes a claim, the source is one click away, in the product and on the landing page.
- The product is the picture: real interface fragments carry the visuals, not illustrations.
- The sender's words stay theirs: generated text is always visibly marked as generated.
- Quiet until it matters: restrained surfaces, with color reserved for sending, sources, and status.
- Honest numbers: cost and limits are shown as measured, never rounded down.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA contrast for all text including placeholders, visible keyboard focus, reduced-motion alternatives for every animation, and status never conveyed by color alone.
