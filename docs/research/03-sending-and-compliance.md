# Gmail/Outlook outreach tool: technical + compliance research (as of 2026-10-10)

## 1. Gmail sending via API/OAuth

**Account sending limits (these govern, not API quota).**
- Consumer @gmail.com: Google triggers "You have reached a limit for sending mail" at "more than 500 emails sent in a day" or >500 recipients in one message; lockout clears "within 1 to 24 hours"; many bounces/invalid addresses trigger a separate 24-hour block. No published numbers for new accounts. https://support.google.com/mail/answer/22839
- Workspace (paid): 2,000 messages/day (1,500 for mail merge; 500 for trial accounts), 10,000 total recipients/day, 3,000 external recipients/day, 3,000 unique recipients/day (2,000 external; 500 external on trial). 500 recipients/message via Gmail API vs 100 via SMTP/IMAP. Exceeding = "can't send new messages for up to 24 hours"; spam senders "might be permanently restricted"; "Sending limits can change without notice." Trial limits only lift after the domain has cumulatively paid $100 (up to 75 days later). https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/gmail/gmail-sending-limits-in-google-workspace
- Gmail API quota (separate): `messages.send` and `drafts.send` cost 100 quota units; limits are 1,200,000 units/min/project and 6,000 units/min/user/project (i.e., ~60 sends/user/minute, far above any sane cold-email rate); 500 recipients/message cap. https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/reference/quota  Note: Google's new "standardized model for agent tools and APIs" changed quotas for projects created on/after May 1, 2026 and says that later in 2026 (90 days' notice) quota-increase requests will require Cloud billing and over-threshold usage will be charged. https://developers.google.com/workspace/tools-safety

**Scopes and verification.**
- Official classification: `gmail.send` = **Sensitive**; `gmail.compose`, `gmail.modify`, `gmail.readonly`, `gmail.insert`, `gmail.metadata`, `https://mail.google.com/` = **Restricted**. "If you store restricted scope data on servers (or transmit), then you must go through a security assessment." https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/auth/scopes
- Practical consequence: **use `gmail.send` only** (plus `userinfo.email`/`openid` for identity). You then need standard app verification (brand verification: homepage on your domain, privacy policy, Search Console domain ownership; plus a demo video and scope justification for sensitive scopes) but **no CASA assessment**. https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13464321  Brand verification "typically takes 2-3 business days"; restricted-scope verification "can potentially take several weeks." https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/restricted-scope-verification
- If you want to read replies or write to Sent/threads with `gmail.modify` or `gmail.readonly`, you're in restricted territory: annual CASA assessment from a Google-empanelled lab, revalidated "at least every 12 months after your assessor's Letter of Assessment (LOA) approval date." Google now assigns AL1 or AL2 assurance levels (old Tier 2/Tier 3 names) "based on user count, requested scopes, and other application-specific signals." https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13465431
- CASA cost (no official price; lab-set): TAC Security historically ~$540/yr (Tier 2) and $4,500 (Tier 3); Leviathan lists AL1 packages at $3,000 / $4,500 / $6,000 depending on start window; other labs $800-$8,000+. https://www.switchlabs.dev/post/casa-tier-2-tier-3-security-review-providers-pricing-and-the-cheapest-option, https://www.leviathansecurity.com/programs/google-casa-cloud-application-security-assessment  Old $15k-$75k figures (GMass 2019 post) are legacy. https://www.gmass.co/blog/google-oauth-verification-security-assessment/

**Shipping a demo without verification (Testing status).** Official rules:
- Publishing status "Testing": "limited to up to 100 test users listed in the OAuth consent screen"; test users see a warning; **authorizations and refresh tokens expire 7 days after consent** (except for apps requesting only openid/email/profile). https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945
- Publishing to "In production" unverified with a sensitive scope: "Unverified app" screen, and a lifetime cap of "100 new users in total, after the app presents the unverified app screen... cannot be reset or changed." https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945, https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/7454865
- Exemptions: "Apps in development/testing/staging mode are not subject to verification" and personal use "fewer than 100 users"; internal-only Workspace apps are exempt from the cap. https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13464323
- Bottom line for the hackathon: Testing mode + judges/team added as test users is fully legitimate; the limits are 100 users and 7-day token expiry (so re-auth weekly).

**Gmail SMTP with an App Password.** Still supported in 2026 but Google says "App passwords aren't recommended and are unnecessary in most cases"; requires 2-Step Verification; not available if 2SV uses only security keys, for Advanced Protection accounts, or (per Google's wording) "You're signed in to a work, school, or other organization account" in some configurations. https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/185833  "Less secure apps" (plain-password IMAP/SMTP) ended for Workspace May 1, 2025, "with the exception of app passwords." https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/sync/transition-from-less-secure-apps-to-oauth  It completely sidesteps OAuth verification, but: SMTP caps at 100 recipients/message, you'd be storing a credential that grants full mailbox access (worse security story than a `gmail.send`-only token), and Workspace admins can block it via 2SV/security-key policy. **Could not verify** a dedicated Admin-console toggle to disable app passwords org-wide (the old "less secure apps" switch was removed).

**How incumbents are verified.** Mailmeteor publishes annual CASA letters: "Assessment Performed by TAC Security," Tier 3, current letter expiring 11/14/2026; earlier letters from NCC Group and TAC (2022, 2025). https://mailmeteor.com/assets/downloads/security/LOA-2026.pdf, https://mailmeteor.com/security  GMass's founder documented the pain of the process. https://www.gmass.co/blog/google-oauth-verification-security-assessment/  **Could not find** public verification details for YAMM or Streak; assume they hold annual CASA letters since they use restricted scopes. Nylas cites "just two weeks" for its own assessment thanks to existing SOC 2 Type II. https://www.nylas.com/blog/google-oauth-app-verification/

## 2. Microsoft Outlook / Graph API

- `Mail.Send` (delegated): "Allows the app to send mail as users in the organization." **Admin consent required: No.** Valid for both personal Microsoft accounts and work/school accounts. https://graphpermissions.merill.net/permission/Mail.Send (mirror of https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/permissions-reference)
- Consent-screen reality: for multitenant apps registered after Nov 8, 2020, "if risk-based step-up consent is enabled, users can't consent to most newly registered multitenant apps that aren't publisher verified" when requesting anything beyond sign-in/profile; the prompt warns the app "was created by an unverified publisher." https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity-platform/publisher-verification-overview  Many tenants also run the `microsoft-user-default-low` policy ("Allow user consent for apps from verified publishers, for selected permissions"); Mail.Send is not low-impact by default, so corporate M365 users will often hit an admin-approval wall. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/enterprise-apps/configure-user-consent
- Publisher verification is **free** ("Microsoft doesn't charge developers for publisher verification"), takes "minutes" once prerequisites are met, but requires a verified Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program (ex-MPN) account, a work/school tenant, a verified publisher domain (not *.onmicrosoft.com), and MFA. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity-platform/publisher-verification-overview
- Sending limits, Exchange Online (M365 business): 10,000 recipients/day per mailbox, 30 messages/minute, customizable 1-1,000 recipients/message; "Exchange Online isn't suited to accommodate bulk-mailing scenarios"; tenant-wide external recipient cap (TERRL) with trial tenants capped at 5,000 external/day. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/office365/servicedescriptions/exchange-online-service-description/exchange-online-limits  Graph per-mailbox throttle: 10,000 requests/10 min, 4 concurrent. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/throttling-limits
- Outlook.com consumer: Microsoft 365 subscribers get 5,000 recipients/day, 500/message, 1,000 "non-relationship" recipients/day; free accounts "lower," unpublished, and new accounts get "a low sending quota" that is "a temporary restriction." https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/sending-limits-in-outlook-com-279ee200-594c-40f0-9ec8-bb6af7735c2e  Third-party estimates for free accounts are ~300/day (unverified).

## 4. Transactional providers' policies on cold outreach

All six prohibit it; quotes:
- **Resend**: "You are prohibited from sending unsolicited messages of any kind, including cold outreach, purchased lists, or scraped contact data." Complaint rate <0.08%, bounce <4%, "account may be shut down without warning." https://resend.com/legal/acceptable-use
- **SendGrid/Twilio**: prohibits "Sending unsolicited or unwanted emails in bulk," "Using purchased or rented email lists or email lists of recipients that have not affirmatively consented," and "Sending emails to email addresses that you obtained from the Internet or social media or to generic email aliases (e.g., webmaster@domain.com or info@domain.com) without obtaining prior affirmative consent." https://www.twilio.com/en-us/legal/service-country-specific-terms/email
- **Postmark**: "All email lists contained and/or used with respect to the Service must be permission-based subscriptions. Use of a list that has been purchased or rented from a third party is prohibited." Complaint rate <0.1%, bounce <10%. https://postmarkapp.com/terms-of-service
- **Mailgun (Sinch)**: "Use of contact lists that are bought, rented or scraped from third-parties is prohibited by law in most countries, and is absolutely prohibited on Sinch Email servers"; marketing mail "can only be sent to recipients who have granted clear, explicit and provable consent... through a confirmed single or double opt-in system." https://www.mailgun.com/legal/aup/
- **Amazon SES (AWS AUP)**: may not use services "to distribute, publish, send, or facilitate the sending of unsolicited mass email or other messages, promotions, advertising, or solicitations (or 'spam')." https://aws.amazon.com/aup/
- **Loops**: "You agree that you will only send targeted, permission-based messages"; "Purchased lists may not be used with the Services, regardless of the source or permission status"; recipients "must have registered with your software product." https://loops.so/terms

Conclusion: an ESP is not an option for outreach; sending from the user's own mailbox is the only architecture consistent with these terms.

## 5. Deliverability requirements

- **Google (Feb 2024, enforcement ramping Nov 2025)**: All senders to personal Gmail: SPF or DKIM, PTR, TLS, "Keep spam rates reported in Postmaster Tools below 0.10% and avoid ever reaching a spam rate of 0.30% or higher." Bulk senders (5,000+/day to Gmail): SPF + DKIM + DMARC (p=none OK), aligned From, one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058 headers `List-Unsubscribe` + `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) honored within 2 days. https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126  FAQ: "A bulk sender is any email sender that sends close to 5,000 messages or more to personal Gmail accounts" in 24h; "permanently considered bulk senders"; "Starting November 2025, Gmail is ramping up its enforcement on non-compliant traffic," including permanent rejections; guidelines "don't apply to messages sent to Google Workspace accounts." https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414
- **Yahoo**: same shape; bulk senders need SPF+DKIM, DMARC p=none, RFC 8058 list-unsubscribe, "Honor unsubscribes within 2 days," spam rate <0.3%; no published volume threshold. https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/
- **Microsoft (May 5, 2025)**: domains sending >5,000/day to Outlook.com/Hotmail/Live: SPF pass, DKIM pass, DMARC "At least p=none and align with either SPF or DKIM"; non-compliant mail junked, with rejection ("550; 5.7.515 Access denied") to come on an unannounced date. https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftdefenderforoffice365blog/strengthening-email-ecosystem-outlook%E2%80%99s-new-requirements-for-high%E2%80%90volume-senders/4399730

**Does it apply to 30/day from your own Gmail?** The bulk-sender tier does not. The "all senders" tier is satisfied automatically: Gmail/Workspace sign SPF/DKIM for you, and @gmail.com has DMARC. Postmaster Tools only shows compliance data at bulk volume. What actually hurts low-volume accounts (consensus of vendor sources, not Google-documented thresholds): fresh account/domain sending at volume immediately, high bounce rate (Google's own page says many bounces trigger a 24h block), spam complaints, identical bodies to many recipients, link-heavy/HTML-heavy content, rapid-fire sends, and server-IP logins. https://support.google.com/mail/answer/22839, https://mailmeteor.com/blog/gmail-blocked-my-account, https://coldmailer.ai/blog/can-you-send-cold-email-from-gmail  Gmail Program Policies: "Don't use Gmail to distribute spam or unsolicited commercial mail"; no "Creating or using multiple accounts... intended to engage in a behavior that was previously prohibited." https://support.google.com/mail/answer/16734397

**Warm-up guidance** (vendor consensus, 2026): 3-10/day in week 1, ramping ~20% day-over-day over 4 weeks to a ceiling of 30-40 (max ~50) cold emails per mailbox per day; add mailboxes rather than exceed that. https://leadhaste.com/blog/email-warmup-timeline-and-schedule, https://syncgtm.com/blog/warm-up-email-address, https://www.smartlead.ai/blog/email-warmup-guide  Specific inter-send delay recommendations weren't found in authoritative sources; the Exchange cap of 30/min and Gmail's 6,000 units/min are far above any safe pace.

## 6. Legal

- **CAN-SPAM (US)**: "The law makes no exception for business-to-business email." Requirements: no false/misleading headers, no deceptive subject lines, identify the message as an ad, "valid physical postal address," clear opt-out, honor opt-outs "within 10 business days," opt-out mechanism must work "for at least 30 days after you send your message," and "Monitor what others are doing on your behalf." Penalty "up to $53,088" per email. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business
- **US states**: Washington CEMA now carries a $500/email statutory penalty for any false or misleading subject line after *Brown v. Old Navy* (Apr 2025), with ~100 suits since; California B&P 17529.5 allows private suits up to $1,000/email for deceptive headers/subjects. https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/washington-supreme-court-expands-email-subject-line-restrictions-under-cema  Practical rule: never use fake "Re:"/"Fwd:" or misleading subjects.
- **UK (PECR + UK GDPR)**: ICO: "The PECR rule on direct marketing by electronic mail does not apply to corporate subscribers"; you must not conceal identity, must "give a valid address for business to opt-out," should honor opt-outs, and still need a UK GDPR lawful basis ("legitimate interests will be the appropriate lawful basis" in many cases, with a three-part test). Sole traders/some partnerships are individual subscribers needing consent or soft opt-in. Guidance "under review" due to the Data (Use and Access) Act. https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/
- **EU**: GDPR Recital 47 treats direct marketing as a possible legitimate interest, but national ePrivacy transpositions govern whether you may email at all: Germany (UWG §7) requires prior express consent even B2B; France is permissive for role-relevant B2B with disclosure + opt-out; Spain requires consent/soft opt-in; Netherlands reported permissive. Sources here are vendor blogs; **treat as unverified** and avoid Germany by default. https://overloop.com/blog/b2b-cold-email-germany-gdpr-compliance, https://prospeo.io/s/gdpr-cold-email-b2b
- **Canada (CASL)**: implied consent for a "conspicuously published" business address only if there's no no-solicitation statement and the message is relevant to the recipient's role; sender bears burden of proof; every CEM must identify sender, give contact info, and include an unsubscribe that works for 60 days and is honored within 10 business days; penalties up to $10M per violation for businesses. Practitioner source: https://substancelaw.ca/casl-compliance/ ; statute https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/index.html (**CRTC FAQ pages returned 403; statute not fetched directly**).

**Minimal compliant footer**: sender's real name, company, and a valid physical postal address; a one-line opt-out ("Reply 'unsubscribe' or click here and I'll remove you") that stays functional 60 days; accurate From/Reply-To; for CASL/GDPR, a one-line "why you're receiving this" (e.g., "I found your role at X on your company site"). Avoid "ADV" tags only if the message is clearly commercial by nature; the FTC says it must be identified as an ad.

## 7. Tracking

Open pixels force HTML, are inflated by Apple MPP, and on shared tracking domains tie your reputation to strangers; rewritten links "look identical to a phishing redirect" to secure email gateways. Hunter: "never track clicks. Never." Open tracking "okay" only with a custom tracking domain. https://hunter.io/cold-email-guide/email-tracking, https://instantly.ai/blog/email-tracking-and-deliverability-why-tracking-pixels-can-hurt-your-inbox-placement/  **MVP recommendation: skip both; measure replies** (which `gmail.send` + a reply-forward or user-reported outcome can capture without restricted scopes).

GMass on unsubscribe for small senders: List-Unsubscribe header in cold email "Probably not, unless you're a bulk sender"; "bake that option into your message" in plain text, and make the visible link obvious. When enabled, GMass inserts both mailto and URL variants plus `List-Unsubscribe-Post: one-click`, and auto-suppresses unsubscribed addresses before each campaign. https://www.gmass.co/blog/list-unsubscribe-header/

## Synthesis

### A. Lowest-friction hackathon architecture (1-2 days)
1. Own Google Cloud project, OAuth consent screen in **Testing**, scopes `gmail.send` + `openid email`. Add judges/team as test users (cap 100; tokens expire in 7 days, which is fine). No verification needed. Store only the refresh token encrypted; never store mail content.
2. Send with `users.messages.send` (100 quota units each; never approaches 6,000/min). Draft with the LLM, **human approves each email or batch** before send; the app never sends autonomously.
3. Outlook: register a multitenant Entra app with delegated `Mail.Send`; test with a personal outlook.com account (no admin consent) and note that corporate tenants may require admin approval until publisher-verified.
4. Skip SMTP/app passwords (full-mailbox credential, worse story) and skip ESPs (all prohibit cold email).
5. What to tell judges: least-privilege scope (send-only, can't read mail); Google-classified "Sensitive," not "Restricted," so no CASA needed; human-in-the-loop send; hard-coded per-user caps far below Gmail's 500/day; built-in footer, suppression list, and bounce handling; no tracking pixels or link rewriting.

### B. Path to a real product
- Google: complete brand verification (2-3 business days: homepage, privacy policy, Search Console) and sensitive-scope verification (demo video, justification); keep to `gmail.send` to avoid CASA. Cost: $0. Timeline: ~1-4 weeks. If later needing `gmail.readonly/modify` for reply detection, budget an annual CASA (AL1 roughly $540-$6,000/yr depending on lab; AL2 $4,500-$8,000+) plus several weeks, every year.
- Microsoft: publisher verification (free, needs a Partner Center account and verified domain) so users in default-policy tenants can self-consent; otherwise expect admin-consent requests.
- Alternative: Composio managed auth (free tier, inherits their verified app) gets live immediately, at the cost of a full-mailbox consent screen branded "Composio" and shared-app suspension risk.

### C. Compliance checklist
- Footer: real name + company + physical postal address; plain-text opt-out line and link; "why you're receiving this" line (data source); accurate From/Reply-To; no misleading subject lines (WA/CA private suits).
- Unsubscribe: honor immediately (law allows 10 business days; Google/Yahoo expect 2 days); link functional 60+ days (CASL) and at least 30 days (CAN-SPAM); global per-user suppression list checked before every send; also parse "unsubscribe"/"remove me" replies.
- Suppression list: unsubscribes, hard bounces, complainers, and a do-not-contact import; block generic aliases (info@, sales@) by default.
- Geography gating: warn/block for German recipients; for EU/UK, require the user to confirm a role-relevant B2B rationale (legitimate-interest record) before sending.
- Rate limiting and content: defaults below; require per-recipient variation; no identical bodies; limit links (ideally 0-1) and no attachments/images; plain text or minimal HTML; no open/click tracking.
- Account hygiene: refuse to run on accounts <2 weeks old without warm-up mode; pause the campaign automatically if bounce rate >3% or any spam complaint signal; one mailbox per user, no account rotation (violates Gmail policies).
- Record-keeping: log consent basis / data source per contact, send timestamps, and opt-outs (CASL burden of proof; CAN-SPAM "monitor what others are doing on your behalf").

### D. Recommended default sending limits for a new user
- New/unwarmed mailbox: 10/day week 1, 20/day week 2, 30/day week 3, 40/day from week 4; never increase >20% day-over-day (vendor consensus; Google publishes no safe-volume figure).
- Established mailbox hard cap: **50/day** consumer Gmail, **100/day** Workspace/M365 (user-raisable to 150 with a warning); these sit at 10%/5% of Google's 500/2,000 account caps and inside Microsoft's 1,000 "non-relationship" daily cap for consumer subscribers.
- Hourly: max 10-12/hour; delay between sends randomized **60-180 seconds** (jitter, not fixed), within business hours of the recipient's timezone. This is also under Exchange's 30/min and Gmail's quota by two orders of magnitude.
- Recipients per message: 1 (never CC/BCC lists).
- Auto-pause triggers: bounce rate >3% in a campaign, any "reached a limit" / 5.7.x response from Gmail, or 429/`MailboxQuotaExceeded`-class errors from Graph.

**Unverified / not fetched:** Nylas Google-provider docs (404), CRTC CASL FAQ (403; statute cited by URL only), YAMM/Streak verification specifics, free Outlook.com numeric limits (Microsoft publishes none), EU national ePrivacy specifics (vendor blogs only), and all warm-up/delay numbers (practitioner consensus, not platform policy).
