# Envoy demo video plan

For Jiwoo and Adam. What to record, what to say, and what proof to put on screen so the judges believe every claim.

**Assumed length: 3 minutes.** If the limit is different, see "If you have more or less time" at the bottom.

## The one idea the video has to land

> Outreach that used to take an evening per company now takes a minute, and every sentence Envoy writes comes with a link to the page it came from.

Two things make judges believe that. They watch it happen in real time, not as slides. And they see receipts: real source pages, a real email arriving in a real Gmail inbox, and the real cost on screen.

## How the rubric maps to the video

| Criterion | Weight | What proves it on screen | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem and insight | 20% | Adam's real hand-written Lightship email and the spreadsheet or steps it took to make it | 0:00–0:25 |
| Product and demo | 25% | One unbroken journey: goal, organizations, brief, contacts, draft, sent email in the inbox | 0:25–2:15 |
| Technical execution | 20% | Clicking a citation opens the real page; "Still unknown" section; "How this brief was checked"; "Email not checked" vs "No email" | 1:00–1:45 |
| Commercial potential | 20% | The cost line ("Spent so far: $…") and a named BEACN person or club who will use it this semester | 2:15–2:40 |
| Team and pitch | 15% | What changed after Adam used it on real outreach (10 contacts, edit each draft, no postal address) | 2:40–3:00 |

## Shot list

Each row is one shot. The **Say** column is a suggested voice-over line; rephrase freely, but keep it about as short.

### 1. The real problem (0:00–0:25)

| Show | Say |
|---|---|
| Adam's actual Lightship email in Gmail Sent, then the spreadsheet or tab mess used to find the contact | "This is a real email I sent to win a consulting project. Finding the right person, their email, and something specific to say took [Adam: real time per company]. Our club sends [Adam: real number] of these a semester." |
| Quick cut of the steps: LinkedIn, company site, Hunter, spreadsheet, GMass | "Five tools, one company at a time." |

Why it wins: the rubric literally asks for "a founder's real example". Use the real email, not a mock-up. Blur the recipient's address.

### 2. Setup takes one click (0:25–0:45)

| Show | Say |
|---|---|
| Setup page. Type the BEACN website, click **Write the rest from my website** | "You give Envoy your website once." |
| The email box fills in. Point at the yellow highlights: name, title, organization | "Yellow is your details, filled in automatically. Green changes for each person. Blue is the part Envoy researches and writes. Everything else is your own words." |
| Signature preview with BEACN logo and the linked organization name | (no line needed) |

Pre-upload the BEACN logo (`~/Downloads/logo.png`) so it already shows.

### 3. A goal becomes a list (0:45–1:00)

| Show | Say |
|---|---|
| New campaign. Click **Use this example** or type the clean-energy goal. People per company: 10. Click **Find organizations** | "I describe who I want to work with, in plain English." |
| The loading screen with progress steps | "Envoy searches the web for organizations that match, and skips anyone we've already contacted." |
| The list of organizations, each with a one-line reason and a **Source** link | "Every suggestion comes with the reason and where that reason came from." |

Speed this up if the search takes longer than 10 seconds, and put a small "sped up 4x" label in the corner. Honest speed-ups are more convincing than hidden cuts.

### 4. The brief, with receipts (1:00–1:45). This is the wow moment.

Use a company you pre-researched (see the checklist). Spend the most time here.

| Show | Say |
|---|---|
| Brief header: profile and fit score | "For each organization, Envoy reads their site and recent news and writes a one-page brief." |
| **Fit against your criteria**: click one superscript number. The real source page opens in a new tab. Highlight the matching sentence on that page | "Every claim has a number. Click it and you land on the exact page it came from." |
| **What both sides gain**: point at an "Evidenced" badge next to an "Inferred" one | "It tells you what's proven and what's a reasonable guess." |
| **Still unknown** | "And it tells you what it couldn't find, instead of making it up." |
| Open **How this brief was checked** | "Every citation is checked by code against the pages it read. Quotes are matched word for word. If a brief fails, a stronger model rewrites it." |

Why it wins: this is the "Technical execution" criterion word for word: accurate extraction, working source links, sensible handling of missing information.

### 5. Ten people, not one (1:45–2:00)

| Show | Say |
|---|---|
| **Who to write to**: the list of about 10 people with titles, LinkedIn and Source links | "Envoy finds the people in the roles you picked, often several per role." |
| Click **Find email** on one person. Its badge changes to show whether the address is verified | "Emails are only looked up for people you want, so you don't pay for the rest." |
| Tick two or three people. The count changes to "3 will get this email" | "Everyone you tick gets their own copy, greeted by name." |

### 6. Your email, made specific (2:00–2:15)

| Show | Say |
|---|---|
| The draft. Point at the blue sourced sentence and the three idea bullets | "Only two parts are written by AI: the opening detail and three project ideas, each tied to a source. The rest is the email you already send." |
| Click into the email, add one sentence, **Save changes** | "You can still edit any draft." |
| Click **Send**. Cut to Adam's Gmail: the email arrives from his own account, with signature, logo and the linked organization name | "It sends from your own Gmail, spaced out and capped, so it reads and lands like an email you wrote." |

For the recording, send only to your own or a teammate's inbox. Never send to a real prospect on camera.

### 7. It costs cents (2:15–2:40)

| Show | Say |
|---|---|
| Campaign header: "Spent so far: $X … about $Y per company" | "This whole campaign cost [read the number off the screen]." |
| One simple slide: who pays and why | "Student consulting clubs, agencies and small founding teams. [Named person] at [club] is using it for this semester's outreach." |

Read the real number from the screen on the day. Don't round it down.

### 8. What we learned, and close (2:40–3:00)

| Show | Say |
|---|---|
| Quick cuts of the three changes below | "We used it on our own outreach first. That told us three things." |
| | "We email about ten people per company, not one. Every draft needs a human edit. And the setup has to make your own details obvious. So we built all three." |
| Logo and one line | "Envoy. Research, a reason to write, and the right person, in a minute per company." |

## Proof checklist: what judges should see with their own eyes

- [ ] A citation number clicked, and the real page open on the matching sentence.
- [ ] An "Inferred" badge next to an "Evidenced" one.
- [ ] A filled-in "Still unknown" section.
- [ ] "How this brief was checked" expanded.
- [ ] A person whose email lookup failed showing "No email", not a guessed address.
- [ ] The live cost line with a real number.
- [ ] The email arriving in a real Gmail inbox, sent from your own account.
- [ ] Adam's real hand-written email at the start.

## Before recording

1. **Run on real keys** at http://localhost:3000, signed in with the Google account you'll send from. Turn off notifications and close other tabs.
2. **Finish Setup.** Fill Your details, run "Write the rest from my website", upload the BEACN logo, and send yourself a test so you know the inbox shot works.
3. **Pre-run one full campaign** with 5 to 10 companies so the brief in shot 4 is ready. Pick the brief with the clearest citations and a useful "Still unknown" entry.
4. **Start a second campaign live on camera** for shots 3 and 5, so the loading screen and fresh results are real.
5. **Click every source link** you plan to show before recording. Some company sites block visitors or change pages.
6. **Get the commercial proof.** A named person at BEACN or another club who will use it this semester. One sentence on camera or a screenshot of the message is enough. Don't invent one.
7. **Fill in Adam's real numbers** in shot 1: time per company today and emails per semester.
8. **Hide anything sensitive.** No `.env.local`, terminal, or API keys on screen. Blur real people's email addresses in the contact list and in Gmail.
9. **Record at 1280 wide or more** with the browser zoomed to 110% so text is readable on a projector.

## Things to avoid

- Don't narrate the stack (Exa, Hunter, Haiku, Next.js). Judges remember what they saw. One line about "checked by code and a second model" is enough tech.
- Don't show a loading screen for more than about 5 seconds without a speed-up label.
- Don't open on a slide. Open on the real email.
- Don't claim a number you haven't seen on the screen that day.
